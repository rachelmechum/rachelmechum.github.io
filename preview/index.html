Research

I study binary black hole mergers with gravitational wave data and population models.

What is a waveform, and what does RIFT do?

I’m studying how the waveform model we use changes the black hole properties we infer from a merger.

A gravitational wave waveform is the shape of a signal over time. As two black holes spiral together, the signal speeds up and grows. It rings down after they merge. LIGO explains gravitational waves here.

RIFT, or Rapid Iterative FiTting, is software that compares a detector signal with computer generated waveforms. It estimates properties such as each black hole’s mass and spin, or rotation. I use RIFT to study how those estimates change when we use different waveform models. I focus on binaries where the black holes have very different masses.

Why are AGN disks interesting for black hole detections?

I’m studying how black holes in AGN disks and embedded star clusters form pairs and merge, and how often we might detect those mergers.

An active galactic nucleus, or AGN, is a bright galaxy center powered by a supermassive black hole and the gas around it. NASA explains AGNs here. Smaller black holes can become embedded in the disk. Some may form pairs and eventually merge, sending out gravitational waves.

I use McFACTS to simulate many possible black hole pairs in AGN disks and embedded star clusters. The models estimate how often they merge and which events detectors might see. Comparing those predictions with observed mergers can help test whether AGN disks are one place black hole pairs form.
