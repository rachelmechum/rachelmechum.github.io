Research

I study binary black hole mergers with gravitational wave data and population models.

What is a waveform, and what does RIFT do?

I’m studying how the waveform model we use changes the black hole properties we infer from a merger.

A gravitational waveform is a computer model of the signal from a black hole merger. We simulate possible waveforms and compare them with signals detected by LIGO. This helps us estimate the black holes’ properties, such as their masses and spins. Click here to read how LIGO explains grav waves.

RIFT, or Rapid Iterative FiTting, is the software I use to compare detected signals with these simulated waveforms. It helps estimate the black holes’ masses and spins. I use it to study how those estimates change with different waveform models, especially for binaries where the black holes have very different masses.

Why are AGN disks interesting for black hole detections?

I’m studying whether black holes in AGN disks and embedded star clusters can form pairs that merge, and how often we might detect those mergers.

An active galactic nucleus, or AGN, is a bright galaxy center powered by a supermassive black hole and the gas around it. Smaller black holes can become embedded in the disk. Some may form pairs and eventually merge, sending out gravitational waves.

I use McFACTS to simulate many possible black hole pairs in these environments. The results help predict which mergers detectors might see. Comparing those predictions with observed mergers can help test whether AGN disks are one place black hole pairs form.
